\section*{Abstract}

Effective vineyard disease management depends on the continuous integration of crop, environmental, disease, and management-action information throughout the crop season. However, recently developed digital tools, including disease-risk prediction, image recognition, and advisory support, remain largely focused on individual functions and application scenarios. Their limited integration of information and workflows across modules restricts their ability to support management throughout the crop season. To address this gap, this study presents GrapeMaster, a scalable digital platform centered on the crop season and designed to integrate these isolated functions into a closed-loop vineyard disease-management workflow. Using the crop season as its organizing unit, the platform integrates field configuration, weather data, phenology simulation, disease-risk interpretation, warning notifications, plant-protection tasks, and treatment feedback, while incorporating symptom image recognition and LLM-assisted advisory support to establish a unified seasonal management process. The deployed platform managed 47 operational accounts, 139 vineyard fields, and 128 crop seasons. A representative workflow replay illustrated the core progression from disease-risk interpretation and event delivery to field operations, treatment feedback, and management review. Platform-compatible analytical modules further supported deployment in humid subtropical vineyards in Guangxi, including downy mildew first-infection warning, broad-stage phenology visualization, and grape disease image recognition. GrapeMaster organizes disease-risk prediction, diagnosis, advisory support, and operational records into a unified, auditable workflow, providing a crop-season-centered organizational framework for continuous disease management in perennial fruit crops.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} grapevine; downy mildew; decision support system; crop-season management; digital agriculture; advisory support; record traceability

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

In subtropical grape-growing regions, environmental conditions characterized by high temperature, high humidity, persistent rainfall, and leaf wetness make downy mildew outbreaks more frequent, less predictable, and more destructive \citep{gesslerPlasmoparaViticolaReview2011,yuEffectsRainShelter2017}. Consequently, these production conditions depend more heavily on efficient and precise vineyard disease management. However, growers' management decisions still rely heavily on past experience, paper-based records, and informal advisory sources of varying reliability, making it difficult to accurately assess disease risk and continuously integrate multi-source information \citep{tummersObstaclesFeaturesFarm2019,yaoInfluenceInformationSources2025}. The inappropriate use of fungicides further increases production costs, reduces control efficacy, and imposes additional environmental pressure \citep{oliverFungicideUsePatterns2023,zubrodFungicidesOverlookedPesticide2019}. Therefore, improving disease-risk identification and management decision making has become an important research objective \citep{gentPerceptionsRiskRisk2011,roseDecisionSupportTools2016}.

In recent years, digital tools targeting key components of disease management have advanced considerably, providing new technical approaches for improving management practices \citep{fountasFarmManagementInformation2015,zhaiDecisionSupportSystems2020}. Downy mildew warning models have evolved from empirical rules to semi-mechanistic and mechanistic models based on pathogen development and infection processes, enabling more accurate characterization of infection risk and improved spray timing \citep{rossiMechanisticModelSimulating2008,caffiEvaluationDynamicModel2011}. Grapevine phenology models describe host development and support the interpretation of susceptible periods and the scheduling of plant-protection operations \citep{parkerGeneralPhenologicalModel2011a,wangAssessingGrapevinePhenological2020}. Meanwhile, image-recognition techniques have substantially improved automatic disease diagnosis, while large language model (LLM)-based advisory systems have further enhanced agronomic interpretation and management recommendation generation \citep{raiAdvancingSitespecificDisease2026,yanCDIPChatGLM3DualmodelApproach2025}. However, these digital tools remain largely focused on individual analytical tasks or application scenarios. Their outputs are typically isolated, lacking sustained information integration and workflow coordination, and are therefore insufficient to support continuous management throughout the crop season \citep{mccownLocatingAgriculturalDecision2002,roseDecisionSupportTools2016}.

Building upon these advances, research has increasingly shifted from developing individual digital tools toward organizing model services, user interaction, and operational management within unified digital platforms \citep{fountasFarmManagementInformation2015,bakirScalableEndtoendIoT2026}. Compared with studies focusing solely on analytical model performance, these platforms place greater emphasis on system architecture, data organization, deployment, operational services, and user-oriented functionality \citep{tummersObstaclesFeaturesFarm2019,bakirScalableEndtoendIoT2026}. In viticulture, vite.net improved practical vineyard disease management by integrating vineyard monitoring, model-based decision support, and bidirectional communication, whereas MISFITS-DSS implemented regional-scale downy mildew risk assessment for public plant-protection services \citep{rossiAddressingImplementationProblem2014,bregaglioPublicDecisionSupport2022}. Beyond viticulture, agricultural platforms such as Fuxi Brain further integrate Internet of Things (IoT) sensing, multimodal data fusion, model services, and intelligent advisory functions to support digital management throughout the agricultural production process \citep{chenAgriculturalAutonomousDecisionmaking2026}. These studies indicate that agricultural digital platforms are evolving from collections of analytical models into integrated service systems for production applications. The necessity of integrating individual digital tools into unified digital platforms has therefore become widely recognized in agricultural production \citep{romera2024digitalization}.

Despite the continuous development of digital tools and digital platforms, disease risk continues to evolve throughout the crop season, making improvements in individual analytical capabilities or platform functionality alone insufficient to meet practical disease-management needs \citep{mccownLocatingAgriculturalDecision2002,roseDecisionSupportTools2016}. The remaining challenge is no longer the development of additional digital tools, but the organization of existing digital capabilities into a continuous disease-management workflow spanning the entire crop season to accommodate the long-term and dynamic nature of disease occurrence in perennial fruit crops \citep{fountasFarmManagementInformation2015,romera2024digitalization,bakirScalableEndtoendIoT2026}. A unified and effective organizational unit is still lacking to organize disease-risk prediction, diagnostic analysis, advisory support, and operational records into a unified, continuous, and closed-loop disease-management workflow, thereby supporting dynamic decision making, end-to-end management, and continuous traceability in production practice \citep{mccownLocatingAgriculturalDecision2002,roseDecisionSupportTools2016,rossiSharingDecisionmakingTools2023}.

To address this challenge, we designed and deployed GrapeMaster, a crop-season-centered digital platform for vineyard disease management. The platform is organized around the complete grape crop season, from budburst to fruit maturity and harvest, which serves as the fundamental unit for management activities and multi-source data organization. Field background information, weather data, phenology simulation, disease-risk interpretation, warning notifications, plant-protection tasks, treatment feedback, symptom image recognition, LLM-assisted advisory support, and historical management records are integrated into a unified disease-management workflow. This paper presents the architecture, core implementation modules, deployment, and user-oriented disease-management workflow of GrapeMaster. It demonstrates how existing digital analytical capabilities can be organized around the crop season to establish a continuous disease-management platform for practical production and provides a reusable, extensible, crop-season-centered platform architecture for digital disease management in perennial fruit crops.

\section{System architecture}\label{system-architecture}

The system architecture was developed around three design requirements. First, the platform distinguishes persistent vineyard fields from annually renewed crop-season management units to support management based on the crop season for perennial fruit production. Second, heterogeneous records, including weather, phenology, disease risk, notifications, tasks, treatment feedback, symptom images, advisory records, and messages, are organized and retrieved through direct or indirect linkage within the crop-season management context. Third, disease-risk interpretation remains connected to warning delivery, plant-protection tasks, treatment feedback, and management review, enabling disease management to be organized as a continuous and traceable disease-management workflow rather than as isolated analytical outputs. The following sections describe the platform organization and operational mechanism from four perspectives: conceptual architecture, logical architecture, service architecture, and analytical services.

\subsection{Conceptual Architecture}\label{conceptual-architecture}

The conceptual architecture organizes the platform around the crop season (Figure~\ref{fig:crop_season_traceability_architecture}). The crop-season unit serves as the central organizational context linking persistent field information, environmental and phenological state, analytical services, management services, and historical records. Symptom-image recognition and LLM-assisted advisory support are retained as an auxiliary branch within the same seasonal context. Through this organization, environmental information, analytical outputs, management actions, feedback, and review records remain connected within a season-long management loop.

\subsection{Logical Architecture}\label{crop-season-object-and-workflow-states}

The logical architecture describes how the platform organizes data objects and business processes around the crop season. Each crop season is used as the basic unit for management activities and multi-source data organization. Shared crop-season, field, task, image, and message identifiers link field information, weather data, phenology state, disease risk, notifications, plant-protection tasks, treatment feedback, symptom images, and advisory records within the crop-season management context. For one crop season \(c\), the management object was represented as:
\begin{equation}
T_c =
\left(
F_c, M_c, W_c, P_c, R_c, N_c, O_c, B_c, E_c, A_c
\right),
\label{eq:crop_season_traceability_object}
\end{equation}
where \(F_c\) is the linked field record, \(M_c\) is crop-season metadata, \(W_c\) is weather input, \(P_c\) is phenology state, \(R_c\) is disease-risk output, \(N_c\) is event-delivery records, \(O_c\) is task or field-operation records, \(B_c\) is treatment-feedback records, \(E_c\) is symptom or image records, and \(A_c\) is advisory or message records. These objects jointly define the crop-season management context. Weather, phenology, and disease risk define analytical states. Notifications and tasks define business states. Treatment feedback updates management states, and symptom images and advisory records provide post-symptom and advisory branches. The corresponding record groups and traceability functions are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:traceability_object}.

The platform defines business state-transition rules around these objects. Weather and phenology updates support disease-risk interpretation, and disease-risk states trigger warnings and notifications. Notifications can be converted into plant-protection tasks, and task completion generates treatment feedback. Treatment feedback can then be used to update subsequent protection-state interpretation and disease-risk review. All state changes are retained within the same crop-season management context, forming a continuous disease-management workflow from disease-risk interpretation to task execution, feedback updating, and management review. The predefined state-transition rules are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:state_transition_rules}.

\subsection{Service Architecture}\label{service-architecture}

The service architecture divides the platform into four layers: application and presentation, backend service, analytical processing, and record management (Table~\ref{tab:architecture_layers}).

The application and presentation layer provides mobile functions for field management, crop-season workspaces, weather and phenology display, risk warning, plant-protection tasks, image upload, and advisory interaction.

The backend service layer manages authentication, API services, scheduled jobs, business objects, and persistent data, thereby supporting platform business logic and data storage.

The analytical processing layer handles weather preprocessing, phenology simulation, disease-risk analysis, and spraying-suitability analysis, and provides unified analytical service interfaces for the platform.

The record management layer manages notifications, tasks, treatment feedback, image-recognition records, advisory messages, and historical records, supporting end-to-end business records and state updates.

\subsection{Analytical Services}\label{analytical-services}

The platform connects analytical services as replaceable components within the unified architecture, and these services operate around the crop-season object (Supplementary Table S1).

The phenology service receives crop-season metadata, cultivar information, and daily temperature series, and outputs date-aligned broad-stage phenology states for disease-risk interpretation, warning-window definition, and plant-protection task scheduling. This study used a Guangxi-calibrated configuration of temperature-driven grapevine phenology modelling rather than proposing a new phenology model \citep{parkerGeneralPhenologicalModel2011a,wangAssessingGrapevinePhenological2020}.

The disease-risk service receives weather data, phenological stages, cultivar susceptibility, target-disease configuration, and recent treatment history, and calls a locally calibrated first seasonal infection model for subtropical vineyards (FSIM-S) to generate date-aligned disease-risk states, warning triggers, treatment windows, and protection-state interpretation (Figure~\ref{fig:disease_risk_engine_structure}). In the current platform, FSIM-S is used as a first-infection warning module for humid subtropical vineyards. Its infection-process logic is derived from published downy mildew models and calibrated with Guangxi data, with technical details summarized in Supplementary Table S1 \citep{rossiMechanisticModelSimulating2008,caffiModelPredictingPrimary2009c,caffiEvaluationDynamicModel2011,brischettoWeatherDrivenModelPredicting2021}.

Image recognition and advisory services are analytical branches embedded within the same disease-management workflow centered on the crop season. The image-recognition module receives user-uploaded field images and outputs disease categories and confidence values. The advisory module combines user questions with available diagnostic information to generate management-oriented recommendations and stores advisory records. Both services follow the published CDIP-ChatGLM3 framework and are used only as auxiliary analytical services for disease management. They do not replace the disease-risk engine, expert diagnosis, pesticide labels, or regulated plant-protection decisions \citep{yanCDIPChatGLM3DualmodelApproach2025}.

\subsection{Backend service and record storage}\label{backend-service-and-record-storage}

The backend layer manages authentication, API routing, identifier assignment, relational storage, scheduled jobs, and reference-table lookup. It stores the main business objects required by the platform, including accounts, fields, crop seasons, weather payloads, analytical outputs, notifications, tasks, treatment feedback, image-analysis records, advisory messages, and field notes. Standardized reference tables support grape variety, cultivation method, BBCH stage, disease type, fungicide list, pesticide list, and reminder rules. The output of this layer is a set of operational records linked by account, field, crop-season, task, image, or message identifiers, supporting later retrieval under the same crop-season unit (Supplementary Figure S1 and Supplementary Tables S2 and S6).

\subsection{Weather and phenology processing}\label{weather-and-phenology-processing}

Weather processing uses field coordinates and active crop-season identifiers to retrieve historical and forecast weather data from the Open-Meteo weather API (\url{https://open-meteo.com/}). Scheduled backend workflows convert daily mean temperature into phenology input, hourly weather into disease-risk input, and short-term hourly records into spraying-suitability variables. Missing values in date-aligned weather series are filled by linear interpolation before analytical use. The outputs are crop-season-linked weather payloads and transformed variables for downstream services.

Phenology processing then receives crop-season metadata, cultivar information, and daily temperature series. Broad grapevine phenological states are estimated using the platform-compatible phenology workflow described in Section~\ref{analytical-services}. The outputs are phenological-state records for presentation and a daily phenological-stage sequence for disease-risk interpretation and task timing.

\subsection{Disease-risk service and treatment-feedback workflow}\label{disease-risk-service-and-treatment-feedback-workflow}

The disease-risk service receives transformed weather variables, phenological-stage sequence, cultivar susceptibility, target-disease settings, and recent fungicide-history records through the platform risk interface. For the Guangxi configuration, this interface was connected to FSIM-S, the platform-compatible downy mildew first-infection warning module described in Section~\ref{analytical-services}. The implementation stores model request and response payloads, field-risk states, warning triggers, treatment-window information, and protection-state interpretation under the corresponding crop-season context.

The risk service outputs date-aligned risk states, warning triggers, treatment-window suggestions, and task entry information. The implemented workflow converts these outputs into warning notifications and plant-protection task records. When a task is completed, the execution date and product information are stored as treatment feedback. This feedback can be used to update temporary protected or low-risk management states, reflecting the operational assumption that fungicide applications provide a limited protection window affected by product properties, application timing, host growth, rainfall, and disease pressure \citep{caffiFungicideModelsAre2018,oliverFungicideUsePatterns2023,warnekeEffectFungicideMobility2020}. After the protection period ends, disease-risk interpretation resumes using updated weather, phenology, risk input, and management history. This component implements the operational chain from risk interpretation to notification, task execution, treatment feedback, and management review.

\subsection{Image-recognition and advisory services}\label{image-recognition-and-advisory-services}

The image-recognition module takes user-uploaded symptom images as input. It returns disease or healthy categories with confidence information through the CDIP-ChatGLM3 dual-model framework, which combines a deep-learning image classifier with a fine-tuned ChatGLM3-based advisory model for crop disease identification and management guidance \citep{yanCDIPChatGLM3DualmodelApproach2025}. Its outputs are image-analysis records and recognition-result records for downstream presentation.

The advisory module receives user questions together with available diagnostic or management context. Advisory responses are generated through the advisory component of the CDIP-ChatGLM3 framework, and the resulting advisory session and advisory message records are stored by the backend. These records provide a post-symptom branch for symptom evidence and advisory interaction (Figure~\ref{fig:image_advisory_workflow}). They do not replace the downy mildew risk engine, expert diagnosis, pesticide labels, or regulated plant-protection decisions.

\subsection{Disease-risk workflow and treatment feedback}\label{risk-display-and-notification-delivery}\label{task-execution-and-product-feedback}

GrapeMaster organizes disease-risk outputs into a risk-to-feedback workflow under the selected crop-season context. Date-aligned timelines present disease-risk and field-risk states, including the change from untreated risk interpretation to protected-state updates after fungicide feedback is recorded (Figure~\ref{fig:disease_risk_timeline_result}). The platform function is to keep disease-risk interpretation connected with warning delivery, task creation, treatment feedback, and subsequent risk-state updating rather than treating risk output as a standalone display.

When a disease-risk or management event is generated, GrapeMaster delivers it as a reviewable alert or management reminder and retains the event in the notification archive (Figure~\ref{fig:alert_notification_interfaces_result}). Warning or reminder events can then be converted into plant-protection tasks. Task and product records retain field-operation status, execution timing, fungicide or pesticide entries, dosage, field area, and water-volume information (Figure~\ref{fig:task_management_interfaces_result}). Completed task and product records are stored as treatment feedback and can be used for subsequent protected-state interpretation rather than remaining as isolated operation notes.

\subsection{Symptom recognition and advisory support}\label{symptom-image-and-advisory-functions}

GrapeMaster provides a symptom-recognition and advisory branch for post-symptom evidence collection, auxiliary disease identification, and management-oriented advisory support (Figure~\ref{fig:ai_diagnosis_interfaces_result}). After a symptom image is uploaded, the recognition component returns a grape disease or healthy category with confidence information. In the current configuration, this branch covers multiple grape disease categories rather than only downy mildew. The advisory interface supports follow-up questions on symptom interpretation, disease prevention and control, fungicide-use options, and cultivation-management measures. Symptom images, recognition results, user questions, and advisory responses can be stored within the same management environment as field, crop-season, disease-risk, and task records.

\section{Deployment and Operational-Record Audit}\label{deployment-and-backend-record-audit}

The deployed GrapeMaster system consisted of a mobile client, a Django-based backend service, scheduled jobs, and a relational database. This section audits the operational records exported from that deployment to determine whether the implemented platform retained the objects and linkages required for crop-season-centered workflow reconstruction (Supplementary Table S5).

The backend export contained deployment-related records spanning 2023 to 2026, with field, crop-season, notification, and task records retained across consecutive crop seasons. The mappable subset of field records with crop-season linkage covered multiple vineyard regions, including several subtropical grape-production areas in Guangxi and records from other production regions (Supplementary Figure S2).

All operational records used in this audit were exported from the deployed platform rather than generated from simulated data or manually reconstructed logs. The exported tables were screened to examine whether the business objects and relationships defined in the system architecture were retained in deployable form. Accounts without operational records, or without both field-creation records and crop-season management records, were excluded according to predefined screening and verification rules (Supplementary Tables S3 and S7).

The cleaned operational-record export contained 95 deduplicated registered accounts, of which 47 contained both field-creation records and crop-season management records and were retained as operational accounts. Within these accounts, the platform managed 139 vineyard fields and 128 crop seasons, generating 126 weather records, 126 phenology records, 252 disease-risk and model request--response records, 3,590 notification records, 151 plant-protection or irrigation tasks, and 234 fungicide or pesticide application records. Symptom and advisory records included 94 field notes or disease reports, 2,808 image-analysis records, 1,360 advisory or message records, and 27 forum records (Table~\ref{tab:backend_operational_coverage}). Of the retained field records, 124 had valid coordinates and crop-season linkage and were aggregated into 27 regions for spatial visualization.

A representative crop season containing weather, phenology, disease-risk, notification, completed plant-protection task, and fungicide product records was selected for workflow replay (Figure~\ref{fig:crop_season_replay_loop} and Supplementary Table S4). The replay showed that the selected operational records remained linked within a shared crop-season context. Weather, phenology, and disease-risk records formed a unified environmental and analytical state. Notification and plant-protection task records were retained under the same crop-season context, while fungicide product records were linked to the corresponding field operations through task identifiers. Together, these records formed a management chain covering state updating, disease-risk interpretation, event delivery, task execution, and treatment feedback. No field note or disease-incidence record was available for this case, and this absence was retained as part of the symptom and advisory branch.

Overall, the operational-record audit showed that the deployed platform retained the record types and identifier linkages needed to reconstruct the core risk-to-feedback workflow. These analyses therefore assessed workflow reconstructability and record traceability rather than production efficacy, pesticide-reduction, adoption, or economic outcomes.

\section{Evaluation of Platform-Compatible Analytical Modules}\label{evaluation-of-platform-compatible-analytical-modules}

The platform-compatible analytical modules were evaluated independently to characterize the readiness of the current Guangxi configuration. Unlike the deployment audit in Section~\ref{deployment-and-backend-record-audit}, this section reports module-level validation using Guangxi field observations and a grape disease image dataset (Table~\ref{tab:guangxi_field_data}). These results are not interpreted as evidence of platform deployment effectiveness or production outcomes.

For the Guangxi broad-stage phenology module, the evaluation used 151 valid field observations from six Guangxi site-years. The module reached 58.0\% exact major-stage agreement and 76.0\% user-visible agreement after transition-window display, with a mean absolute stage distance of 0.45 major stages when the sparse post-harvest class was excluded from core metrics (Figure~\ref{fig:phenology_major_stage_validation_result}).

For the downy mildew risk-warning module, the FSIM-S configuration produced a validation mean absolute error of 6.3 days and a validation root mean square error of 6.5 days for first-infection timing (Figure~\ref{fig:core_analytical_support_result}). This result describes the evaluated Guangxi configuration of the platform-compatible risk module rather than the effectiveness of deployed plant-protection decisions.

For the grape disease image-recognition module, the evaluated model produced class-wise F1-scores from 0.926 to 0.998 across seven grape disease categories, with downy mildew reaching an F1-score of 0.986 (Figure~\ref{fig:core_analytical_support_result}). The LLM-assisted advisory component was not included in this module-evaluation section because no independent evaluation result for advisory quality was reported in this study.

\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}

\subsection{Crop season as the organizational anchor for grapevine disease management}\label{crop-season-as-the-organizational-anchor-for-grapevine-disease-management}

For grapevine production, vineyard fields usually remain spatially stable, whereas environmental conditions, phenological stages, disease pressure, and management activities change continuously across annual production cycles. Grapevine phenology and disease pressure are strongly structured by seasonal weather, and these temporal dynamics affect both model interpretation and management timing \citep{nogueirajuniorModellingDynamicsGrapevine2018,bregaglioPublicDecisionSupport2022}. A field-based anchor can preserve stable vineyard identity, location, and background management information, but it cannot by itself distinguish the season-specific weather exposure, crop-development state, disease pressure, treatment history, and feedback status that determine disease-management decisions in a given production year. The crop season therefore provides a more suitable organizational unit for grapevine disease-management activities.

Using the crop season as the shared organizational unit enables season-specific analytical, operational, and symptom-related records to remain associated throughout the production cycle. This role was reflected in the deployed records: 139 vineyard fields were associated with 128 crop seasons, and 126 crop seasons contained linked weather, phenology, disease-risk, and model request--response records. These records indicate that the crop-season object functioned not merely as a date label, but as the temporal management layer that organized annual analytical and management states on top of stable field records. Compared with organizing records around individual model outputs or isolated management events, it preserves the seasonal context needed for later interpretation without requiring manual reconciliation of heterogeneous records. This crop-season organization is consistent with integrative digital-agriculture perspectives that emphasize the need to connect heterogeneous data, models, and decisions within coherent production management structures \citep{romera2024digitalization,bakirScalableEndtoendIoT2026}.

\subsection{From analytical outputs to executable disease-management records}\label{from-analytical-outputs-to-executable-disease-management-records}

Digital technologies for vineyard disease management have progressed considerably in areas such as disease-risk prediction, phenology simulation, image recognition, and advisory support \citep{bregaglioPublicDecisionSupport2022,zhaoIntegratingMachineLearning2024,chenAgriculturalAutonomousDecisionmaking2026}. However, an analytical output has limited management value if it remains separated from the actions, feedback, and records that determine whether disease risk is actually reviewed and acted upon in production. This limitation is especially important for grapevine disease management because, within a single crop season, weather conditions, host developmental stage, disease pressure, and plant-protection actions can interact with each other and update the subsequent management context \citep{rossiAddressingImplementationProblem2014,rossiSharingDecisionmakingTools2023,romeroHumidityHighTemperature2022,bregaglioPublicDecisionSupport2022}.

For example, a fungicide application, especially one with protective activity, can temporarily change the protection status used to interpret subsequent disease risk \citep{caffiFungicideModelsAre2018,warnekeEffectFungicideMobility2020}.

GrapeMaster addresses this problem by converting decision-support outputs into management records that support execution, feedback, and review. In the deployed platform, disease-risk interpretation was connected to warning events, plant-protection tasks, treatment-feedback records, and management-review records rather than stored as an isolated model result. The representative workflow replay showed that disease-risk outputs, notifications, completed tasks, and fungicide-product records could be reconstructed as a connected risk-to-feedback sequence. This record conversion is the practical basis for traceability: it allows the system to examine not only what risk state was produced, but also whether the warning entered a field-operation pathway and whether subsequent treatment feedback was retained for review. In this sense, GrapeMaster extends disease-support tools from result delivery toward auditable disease-management records, consistent with recent work emphasizing implementation, integration, and shared decision-tool delivery in agricultural DSSs \citep{romera2024digitalization,rossiSharingDecisionmakingTools2023}.

\subsection{Modular platform architecture for evolving disease-management systems}\label{modular-platform-architecture-for-evolving-disease-management-systems}

The platform adopts a modular architecture in which phenology simulation, disease-risk prediction, image recognition, and advisory services are implemented as independent analytical services around the platform workflow. This separation between workflow infrastructure and analytical services allows individual models to be recalibrated, replaced, or extended without modifying the underlying management process. Backend records therefore support the platform-level claim of workflow-object and identifier retention, whereas analytical readiness is supported by module-specific validation results. The FSIM-S downy mildew first-infection component achieved a validation mean absolute error of 6.3 days, the broad-stage phenology workflow reached 76.0\% user-visible agreement after transition-window display, and the grape disease image-recognition service produced class-wise F1-scores of 0.926--0.998. These results do not imply that the modules are universally transferable, but they show that different analytical services can be hosted, tested, and updated as platform-compatible components rather than embedded as a single fixed model. Similar modular and scalable design principles have been emphasized in recent agricultural platform studies that integrate sensing, backend processing, analytical services, and user-oriented decision support \citep{bakirScalableEndtoendIoT2026,chenAgriculturalAutonomousDecisionmaking2026}.

Such modularity also supports flexible platform deployment. The current implementation operates using public weather services, scheduled backend processing, and mobile user interaction, while remaining compatible with additional environmental sensors, spore-monitoring systems, phenology observation devices, or future analytical models. This creates a staged deployment pathway: the platform can first run with routinely available field and weather inputs, and then incorporate finer local observations when regional disease pressure, microclimate heterogeneity, or cultivar-specific phenology requires higher-resolution evidence. As analytical methods continue to evolve, this architecture allows platform capabilities to be updated while maintaining a consistent disease-management workflow and data organization. Its contribution is therefore not the claim that one calibrated model can be reused unchanged across regions, but the provision of a stable platform structure into which locally validated models, sensing inputs, and advisory components can be progressively introduced \citep{romera2024digitalization,rossiSharingDecisionmakingTools2023}.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Directions}\label{limitations-and-future-directions}

The results of this study should be interpreted as an evaluation of platform architecture, implementation, deployment, and platform-compatible analytical modules rather than as an assessment of production outcomes. Although GrapeMaster demonstrated the ability to organize a continuous disease-management workflow and operate under practical deployment conditions, its long-term effects on disease control, fungicide use efficiency, economic performance, and user adoption have not yet been evaluated \citep{rossiAddressingImplementationProblem2014,roseDecisionSupportTools2016}.

The analytical modules evaluated in this study represent independent services configured for the current platform rather than fixed components of the platform itself. Because the platform separates analytical services from the underlying management workflow, regional deployment will require local calibration and validation of analytical modules according to weather conditions, cultivar characteristics, disease epidemiology, and management practices \citep{bregaglioPublicDecisionSupport2022}.

Future work should therefore focus on repeated deployment across regions and crop seasons to evaluate long-term production performance, user adoption, and regional adaptability under practical production conditions.

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

This study presented GrapeMaster, a crop-season-centered digital platform for vineyard disease management. By using the crop season as the organizational unit, the platform maintains weather, phenology, disease-risk interpretation, field operations, treatment feedback, and management records within a shared seasonal context, while converting decision-support outputs into executable and traceable management records that support warning, task execution, feedback, and review.

The platform further adopts a modular architecture that separates workflow management from analytical services, allowing disease-risk prediction, phenology simulation, image recognition, and advisory modules to be independently evaluated, updated, and replaced without modifying the overall management process. This architecture provides an extensible organizational framework for integrating analytical services into operational disease-management workflows for perennial fruit crops.

Overall, GrapeMaster demonstrates a platform-oriented approach to organizing continuous vineyard disease management. Future work should focus on repeated regional deployment to evaluate long-term production outcomes, including disease-control effectiveness, fungicide-use efficiency, economic performance, and user adoption.
